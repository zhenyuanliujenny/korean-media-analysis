Korean media exposure and implicit language knowledge

From survey cleaning to statistical interpretation: examining how media exposure relates to Korean language task performance.

Project at a glance

This observational study asks whether people exposed to Korean media perform differently on implicit language tasks, and whether first-exposure age and viewing patterns help explain those differences. Outcomes cover vocabulary, speaker identity, and phonology.

My contribution: I participated in data cleaning, variable construction, R analysis, and statistical modeling, and contributed to survey design and report writing within the dissertation team. The work is collaborative.

Results below are taken from the supplied rendered analysis. The original participant-level CSV is not included, and these estimates have not been recomputed for this portfolio.

The workflow excludes unmatched records, records collected before 14 February 2026, duplicate answer profiles, and cases missing the three modeled controls. The final sample includes 239 media-exposed and 123 non-exposed participants.

Turning survey responses into predictors

Age-specific survey branches identify the earliest reported exposure band. The analysis derives starting age, years of viewing, mean viewing-frequency score, and approximate exposure days. Non-exposed participants receive zero on full-sample exposure variables. These are constructed measures with assumptions, not precise behavioral tracking.

Model design

AnalysisImplementation
Group comparisonWelch’s t-test and pooled-SD Cohen’s d
Full-sample modelsF1 duration → F2 + frequency → F3 + controls; nested-model comparisons
Exposed-only modelsM1 starting age → M2 + duration/frequency → M3 + controls
Interaction and diagnosticsStandardized age × duration interaction; M3 variance inflation factors

The three controls are formal learning, heritage background, and self-study, with -1/+1 effect coding. Gender appears in descriptive preparation but is not included in these model formulas.

Selected findings

Exposure groups differ in unadjusted mean score

The reported group comparison has p = 2.93 × 10−6 and Cohen’s d = 0.477. These means are descriptive and unadjusted; they do not estimate a causal benefit of media exposure. No error bars are shown because this chart presents only reported means.

The source reports M3 VIFs between 1.080 and 1.349. This diagnostic applies to M3, not to every explored specification. Low VIFs do not establish causal validity or validate all modeling assumptions.

What this analysis can and cannot establish

  • Observational and retrospective data support associations, with possible selection and recall bias.
  • Age-band starting-age estimates and frequency-to-days mappings simplify exposure history.
  • Age and viewing duration are closely related; model specification and the population being analyzed matter.
  • The source explores multiple models. Selected significant p-values should not be read without that exploratory context.
  • Duplicate answer profiles are a screening heuristic, not verified participant identities.
  • The report does not establish that media exposure causes language learning or that a biological age threshold exists.
